2. Step-by-step, with the exact pages you will visit

STEP 0 · 1 hour

Read the 5-page official orientation (do this before you click "register")

  • The federal buyer's guide is split into five short pages: understand the process → prepare → search → bid → get help. Read them in order once; they define every term you'll meet later.
  • Book one free Procurement Assistance Canada seminar. It's the single highest-value free hour available to a first-time bidder.
Go to: Selling to the federal government · Procurement support for businesses · PAC event calendar
STEP 1 · 1–2 hours

Confirm your CRA business number and exact legal name

  • Everything downstream keys off your CRA business number (BN) — one SAP Business Network account per BN. If Mosaic Singularity is incorporated in Ontario, you already have a BN (it's on your HST returns, payroll, or CRA correspondence).
  • Your legal name and head-office civic address must match CRA's registry exactly. A mismatch is the #1 cause of stalled registrations.
  • You do not need a BN to submit a bid — you need it before a contract, standing offer, or supply arrangement can be issued. Still, do it now so award week isn't blocked.
Go to: Register for a CRA business number · verify legal name via the GST/HST Registry Search
STEP 2 · 1 hour

Create your SAP Business Network account (this replaces the old supplier-registration system)

  • CanadaBuys runs on SAP Business Network (you'll still see the old "SAP Ariba" name in places). Register through the CanadaBuys link only — it is what attaches Public Services and Procurement Canada (PSPC) as a customer to your account.
  • Only one account per CRA business number. If more than one exists under your BN, the CanadaBuys Service Desk deactivates the extras — so decide now which inbox owns it (use a shared inbox like contracts@, not a personal address).
  • You'll get an ANID# (Ariba Network ID). Write it down; it identifies you in every government transaction.
  • You no longer chase a separate "Procurement Business Number" — current CanadaBuys guidance keys registration to your CRA business number.
Go to: Register your business (CanadaBuys) · Full step-by-step registration guide · Registration checklist (have this open) · Account access / log in
STEP 3 · 45 min

Answer the Government of Canada questionnaire

  • Inside your account: profile menu → Company Profile → "Customer requested" tab → "Government of Canada / Gouvernement du Canada". Disable pop-up blockers or the questionnaire opens blank.
  • Questions 3–8 are what you need to bid: consent + Code of Conduct, industry, business type and owners/directors (including anyone owning 5%+), optional Indigenous self-identification, optional diversity self-identification, environment questions.
  • Question 9 = full registration: CRA business number plus financial/payment details. Required once you're the successful bidder. Do it early — it's what avoids delays at award, and only the account's main administrator (with access to payment info) can complete it.
  • Question 12 only matters for defence contracts (cyber certification: CPCSC / CMMC). Expect to skip it for civilian work.
Go to: Registration guide — questionnaire section
STEP 4 · ongoing

Set up your opportunity radar

  • Search Tender opportunities by keyword and by UNSPSC category; filter to Open, and to your province.
  • Save each search and subscribe to email/web-feed notifications. Also "follow" individual notices you may bid on — amendments (addenda) are where bids get lost.
  • Use award notices + contract history before you price anything: it shows who wins, at what value, from which department, and how the requirement was worded last time.
  • Watch the request types: RFP (best value), RFQ / ITT (lowest compliant price), RFI (market research — reply anyway, it shapes the eventual RFP), ACAN (advance contract award notice — intended sole source; you can object if you can supply it), and standing offer / supply arrangement calls.
Go to: Tender opportunities · How to search · Search award notices & contract history · Open procurement data
STEP 5 · the strategic step

Get pre-qualified so buyers come to you

  • Standing offers (SO) and supply arrangements (SA) are pre-competed vehicles: you qualify once, then departments buy from you directly or run a mini-competition among qualified suppliers. For a small firm, this is where repeat revenue actually lives.
  • Professional services: PSPC procures many consulting/IT/professional services through its own sourcing tools (including ProServices), with separate registration from the general supplier account. If Mosaic Singularity sells expertise, register there too.
  • Security clearance: if you'll touch protected/classified information or secure sites, screening runs through PSPC's Contract Security Program and takes time (facility + personnel screening). Start it the moment a target opportunity hints at it — never after you win.
  • Controlled goods / defence: only if applicable — registration in the Controlled Goods Program, and cyber certification for DND work.
Go to: Standing offers & supply arrangements · Register in sourcing tools for professional services · Security requirements & Contract Security Program
STEP 6 · per opportunity

Bid — and lose the right way

  • Build a compliance matrix from the solicitation: every mandatory requirement, the exact page/field in your bid that answers it, and the evidence attached. Mandatory = pass/fail; a great solution that misses one line scores zero.
  • Submit early. Portals congest at the deadline, and late = rejected, no exceptions.
  • Use the Q&A / clarification window to ask about ambiguities — and to read competitors' questions, which reveal what the buyer cares about.
  • After award, ask the contracting authority for a debrief on every loss. It's free, it's usually given, and it tells you exactly what to fix. If you believe the process was unfair, the Office of the Procurement Ombud exists for that.
Go to: How to bid · Bidding guidance (PAC) · Office of the Procurement Ombud
STEP 7 · award

Contract, purchase order, and getting paid

  • Award notice appears publicly; you then complete full registration (BN validation) before the contract/SO/SA can issue.
  • The contract and purchase orders flow through SAP Business Network — invoices are matched to the PO there. Set the "email orders to" address to a monitored shared inbox.
  • Standard conditions to expect: insurance certificates (CGL, professional liability for services), security requirements where flagged, conflict-of-interest and Code of Conduct attestations, and payment terms typically net 30 from receipt of a valid invoice.
  • Deliver on time, document everything, invoice exactly against the PO. Federal past performance is checked on future bids.
Go to: Verifying full registration · CanadaBuys support (PO/invoice help)

4. How the money actually works (so you target the right door)

Competitive vs. non-competitive. Above the regulated limits, a bid must be publicly solicited. Under the Government Contracts Regulations (s.6), a contract may be awarded without soliciting bids when the estimated expenditure doesn't exceed $25,000 for goods, $40,000 for other contracts (i.e. services), or $100,000 for construction-related architectural/engineering services — or where only one supplier can perform.
What that means for Mosaic Singularity. Small service buys under $40k can be directed to you if a buyer already knows and trusts you — which is why relationship + pre-qualification beats cold bidding. Above that line you win on the written bid. Both matter; the sequence is pre-qualify, then bid.

6. Traps that cost small businesses their first contract

Paying for "supplier registration". Registration on CanadaBuys / SAP Business Network is free. Third-party services that charge for it, or that sell "government contract leads", are not the official channel and are often reselling what CanadaBuys gives you free.
Chasing the old system. Supplier Registration Information (SRI) and the Procurement Business Number are retired; today it's a CRA business number + one SAP Business Network account, with full registration at question 9. Guides that walk you through "getting your PBN" are out of date.
Non-compliant bids. Missing a mandatory criterion, a required form, a signature, or the stated page limit is a fail — regardless of quality. Build the compliance matrix first, write second.
One account, many hands. A single account per business number means credential control matters: set the main administrator deliberately, keep the recovery email on a company domain, and document the ANID#.
Honest expectation setting. Registration takes hours; a first award typically takes months and several bids. There is no shortcut and no insider channel — the durable edge is being pre-qualified, compliant, and known to two or three buyers in your niche.
